# Actor-relative foreground fading

Historical geometry note (2026-10-19): [ADR-0015](../adr/0015-use-colliders-for-foreground-fading.md) removes all alpha-derived fade geometry, SpriteGeometry, generated occluder assets, and their tasks/dependencies. Fading uses active wall/door colliders plus full artwork rectangles; lighting uses active object colliders under ADR-0014. Pipeline instructions, silhouette-specific behavior, and measurements below describe earlier implementations. Current setup is in [README](../../README.md), and current behavior is in [CONTEXT](../../CONTEXT.md).

Researched and implemented on 2026-10-14 for the Ritual Gate's southern walls, ceiling caps, and door. The simplified rules and math are recorded in [ADR-0006](../adr/0006-simplified-foreground-fading.md), with scenery-padding rejection in [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-alpha-derived-scenery-bounds.md); the frame interface remains in the [render-data decision](../adr/0005-render-data-and-room-preview.md#render-data-interface). Status lives in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

## Primary-source evidence

- The installed DragonRuby 7.21 SDK documents sprite `a` in the range 0–255, default 255, and default alpha blending in `$DRAGONRUBY_HOME/docs/api/outputs.md`, under **Sprites → Blending**. Ordinary primitive alpha is sufficient; a shader is unnecessary for whole-sprite fading.
- Traditional camera-near fading concerns surface distance from a 3D camera. Epic describes that behavior in its [opacity material functions](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/opacity-material-functions-in-unreal-engine). In this game, the useful distance is between actor feet and foreground artwork in world space.
- Separately fading overlapping sprites can make their overlap more opaque than either sprite alone. Godot explicitly documents group compositing as a remedy in [CanvasGroup](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_canvasgroup.html). Direct per-sprite alpha is the chosen first implementation; additional compositing needs visual evidence.
- [Tiled custom properties](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/custom-properties/) support explicit authoring metadata, including tile defaults overridden on instances. This remains an alternative if automatic geometry proves too permissive.

## Project-specific approach and limits

The geometry rule and distance curve are a project design, not an engine-prescribed algorithm. [ADR-0006](../adr/0006-simplified-foreground-fading.md) records the current local-column eligibility and the invariant allowing point-to-rectangle distance to simplify to the vertical gap above the footprint's top edge. Continuous world Y remains necessary because the stepped southern wall can cover actors sharing its base's grid row.

Player frames are 64×64 with 24 source pixels of foot padding, whereas map tiles are 32×32. Comparing full frame dimensions to identify blockers would reject real wall tiles. Playtesting found that both full-frame and foot-width overlap faded nonblocking neighbors too aggressively. The center line now excludes side padding and grazing contacts; its height is the measured visible artwork envelope above the feet (Player 26 source pixels, Enemy 54), excluding top padding. Source-alpha inspection of the current idle/walk sheets establishes those heights. Supporting-base drawing order prevents overhead caps from incorrectly fading walls behind the actor. Exact silhouettes and per-pixel occlusion remain deferred.

Playtesting found that losing binary eligibility instantly restored opacity, motivating per-unit opacity history. The current `towards` approach bounds change over time and reverses from the current value on reentry; cubic smoothstep and lerp separately shape the distance target. [ADR-0006](../adr/0006-simplified-foreground-fading.md#temporal-approach-towards) explains the distinction and tuning. Multiple actors use the strongest contribution, including on-screen enemies independent of player proximity. This can reveal an enemy's presence through scenery; it does not change future AI sight or lighting rules.

Native regression testing found that DragonRuby 7.21's `Hash#compare_by_identity` returns nil and `Enumerator#to_h` does not perform the requested block transformation. The renderer therefore builds explicit object-ID indices to distinguish equal-looking primitives with different drawing orders.

A later outside-room playtest found false fading beside the vertical side walls and closed side doors. Pixel inspection with DragonRuby's `get_pixels` measured their visible strips as 6×32 inside 32×32 frames. Their open door variants span a full 32×32 alpha bounding rectangle, so tight rectangles alone do not guarantee rejection of internal gaps. [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-alpha-derived-scenery-bounds.md) records the measured-bounds implementation and remaining silhouette limitation.

The user reported the measured-bounds change was almost perfect, then requested that an intersecting member must also be wider than the actor. Body-interval alpha inspection measured maximum widths of 14 source pixels for Player's idle/walk lamp variants and 60 for Enemy's walking poses. [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-alpha-derived-scenery-bounds.md#fade-behavior) records the fixed scaled widths, strict comparison, and consequence for Enemy against 32-pixel units.

## Playtest

Run `bin/rake run`; press R if existing instances need rebuilding after Ruby hot reload. Walk and sneak toward the southern wall, slide sideways along both stepped shoulders, and open/close the southern door with E. Approach and retreat diagonally across adjacent columns.

Check that actors remain readable behind south-side structures, caps and faces fade together, adjacent columns transition acceptably, and overlap does not leave distracting dark patches. Walk outside along both vertical side walls and closed side doors: crossing transparent frame padding or their narrow visible strips should leave them opaque. Check thin caps likewise; a piece no wider than Player's 14-world-pixel body at scale 1 should not trigger fading. Open those doors and inspect contacts around their leaves and corners for false fading inside the tight rectangle. Hug side walls and north-facing corners: background bases, head-padding overlaps, and contacts that miss the actor's center should remain opaque. Step sideways out of a faded column, retreat, and immediately reenter; opacity should restore gradually and reverse smoothly. Repeat at the usual enlarged window size to check pixel alignment and atlas seams. B displays supporting footprints in blue and alpha-derived visible bounds in magenta alongside collision and light diagnostics.

Existing native tests cover geometry, drawing order, distance, multi-actor behavior, immutable artwork, door variants, viewport boundaries, and reset/reload. Automated checks do not establish visual feel or fairness; the preview has no spawned Enemy yet.
