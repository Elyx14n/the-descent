# Use collider bases for room lighting

Full projected sprite outlines incorrectly treated tall walls and caps as flat obstacles occupying the floor beneath their artwork. After accepting the one-wall experiment, the user chose its simpler lighting model for RoomPreview: use objects' authored colliders as their ground bases, remove the separate LightingPreview, keep the player's radius at 64, and default other sources to radius 48. Height-aware shadows are deferred.

Room exposes one light blocker per active object collider, including each rectangle of a multipart base. Doors use the same open/closed collision state as movement. Caps and objects without colliders do not introduce additional ground blockers. This replaces the 2026-10-18 visual-outline lighting policy recorded in ADR-0005. [ADR-0015](0015-use-colliders-for-foreground-fading.md) subsequently adopts active colliders for foreground fading too and removes the generated sprite polygons and magenta diagnostics.

Light calculations use the floor plane. The player source uses the center of its foot collider on the ground plane, keeping it off a collider boundary when touching a southern wall; room sources retain authored X and use their base's lowest Y. Mounted artwork shares the host's bases and source owner exclusion. Mounted sources project one world pixel south/in front of that base: a decorative ledge can be colliderless while a separately owned wall occupies the same cell, so an origin exactly on the base can produce only zero-area floor wedges. This clearance preserves the underlying wall as a blocker, including for the floor behind it, rather than excluding unrelated wall owners. Positive authored radii override the room fallback; raw authored values remain intact in Room's source data. If a receiver has no collider, its placement baseline is a receiving-only fallback and creates no caster.

Lighting prepares all enabled source polygons, each bounded by a 32-sided circle. Render applies their accumulated illumination to a floor-only lightmap, then draws foreground sprites with illumination sampled at their bases. Samples use the prepared sight polygon so a near wall face can be lit while a receiver behind it remains shadowed. Caps receive ambient illumination. Foreground wall faces and horizontal doors receive light only from their south/front side. Room identifies the current face tiles by their full-width thin collider at the artwork's lower edge (using closed geometry to identify doors even when open); top-down strips and side walls retain ordinary base sampling. This prevents an exposed corner from brightening an entire face from behind. It adds no height model or new authored property. Tinting preserves authored colors and foreground-fade alpha, with HUD and diagnostics above lighting. These are deliberate approximations; a base sample does not provide detailed shadows across an upright face.

Ambient brightness remains 12%. At the user's request, floor falloff now samples the existing `light_mask.png` alpha through each sight polygon. A 128×128 target converts that black-alpha mask to opaque RGB illumination using built-in blending, preserving the asset. Foreground base shading retains its linear-distance approximation. Passes are prepared in dependency order (`:radial_light`, `:lightmap`, `:scene`); the floor is drawn and multiplied directly in the scene before foreground sprites. This fixes the reproduced one-frame floor delay and removes the extra floor target. Keep HD disabled under ADR-0013. The integrated seven-light pass initially measured about 30 FPS locally when every source was solved each frame. RoomPreview therefore retains one cache of fixed-room polygons, comparing source data, collider bounds/ownership, and circle/default settings. Door or source changes invalidate it; successful reloads reset it. Artwork animation, fading, and the camera do not affect it. The moving player lamp is solved each frame, and Lighting remains stateless. No height textures, shader pipeline, new dependency, or spatial index is introduced. Earlier performance reports used different radii and visual blockers and are historical evidence, not measurements of this integrated workload.
