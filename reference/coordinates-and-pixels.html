Space tells you what the position is relative to

RepresentationVerbal modelWhat changes the numbers?
World spaceThe actor’s place in the room.Moving the actor through the room. Moving the camera alone leaves this position intact.
Screen spaceThe actor’s place inside the viewing window.Moving the actor or moving the camera window across the room.
Draw spaceThe artwork’s prepared placement at its drawing destination.The selected drawing policy: aligned scenery inside a target, or aligned actor bounds at the final screen destination.

All three use logical units in this translation-only camera. Changing the frame of reference does not require changing the ruler. Draw space is our project term, not an independent universal SDK space.

Source: Camera conversions and snapshots, Render placement.

The ruler and the image grids

TermCategoryWhere it belongs
Logical pixelDistance unit.The ruler used by world, screen, and prepared drawing coordinates here. Distances and positions may be fractional.
Source/artwork pixelAsset image cell.The source picture. Source crops address this image’s coordinates, not locations in the room.
Native raster pixelRendered image cell.A grid in which DragonRuby stores the rendered result. Final output and intermediate scene targets each have their own grids.
Physical/display pixelDisplay output cell.The physical grid onto which the completed game image is enlarged and positioned.

Image grids can have coordinates too. A pixel type names a cell or a unit; that name alone does not tell you whether a position is in world space or screen space.

Sources: project definitions, source and destination rectangles, HD-off configuration, DragonRuby output reference.

Raster is the mosaic; vector is the drawing description

A raster represents an image with a rectangular grid of pixel samples. A vector description specifies shapes and paths. Rasterization evaluates drawing geometry on a destination grid.

The same circle can have a vector source and a raster output. A raster may also come from a photo, a sprite, or procedural drawing; it need not originate as vectors or be saved in a file. Vectorization derives shape/path descriptions from image content.

Sources: W3C PNG image model, W3C SVG introduction, Adobe image tracing. Practice: pixel definitions.

Conversion and alignment do different jobs

Conversion: describe the same point from a new origin. A camera translation can be reversed using the same camera snapshot.

Alignment: place the artwork edge on a chosen grid boundary. Nearby ideal edges may share the same aligned drawing edge, so the exact original fraction cannot be recovered from that edge alone.

Align only the final actor bounds after camera translation. Scenery follows a different policy: align inside a padded target, then present that completed image at the fractional camera offset.

Implementation: Camera#for_drawing(snap: false) and Camera#for_compositing, Render.direct_actor. The default camera conversion can still use a snapped offset; check the snapshot supplied by the caller.

Tell the change as a story

Move only the camera. The room remains still. A fixed landmark shifts within the viewing window. Its world position stays the same.

Enlarge only the window. The same native image occupies more of the display. Logical coordinates and source artwork do not acquire new positions or detail.

Align only the artwork. The displayed edge moves to a grid boundary. Simulation and the interpolated world position keep their precision.

Sources: camera behavior, drawing policy, logical presentation.

Symbolic relationships, with their verbal meaning

Move the origin to the camera, then into the viewport

screen = world − camera + midpoint
world  = screen + camera − midpoint

camera is its world-space center, and midpoint belongs to the logical viewport. The inverse undoes those two shifts. This models the fractional presentation path, not the default snapped camera offset.

Travel from an anchor to its edge, then back

left = screen_anchor − anchor_offset
draw_anchor = align(left) + anchor_offset

align is our teaching name for choosing the nearest native grid boundary. The distance from the edge to its anchor is preserved; aligning the anchor itself need not align the edges.

Enlarge the image, then place it after the margin

display = margin + fit × native
native  = (display − margin) / fit

The inverse first removes the display margin, then undoes enlargement. native is an image coordinate; it is not automatically a world position.

Let both image dimensions contribute

new_width  = k × old_width
new_height = k × old_height
new_cells  = k² × old_cells

More columns and more rows both contribute to raster area. This area relationship alone does not predict whole-game time or FPS.

Source-to-destination scale can also stay symbolic: footprint = destination_width / source_width. Growing the destination while keeping the source crop fixed expands each source sample’s destination footprint.

Sources: actor and scene placement, source and destination widths, presentation. Practice: advanced symbolic reasoning.
